How this works
Start here

The hub is the front door to everything AMS. One habit on every device: open the hub, read the tiles, tap the card you need. If the Finance tile shows a missing dot, the Finance shelf is where the work is.

On the Mac, the hub is what AMS Finance.app in the Dock opens: localhost:7780, served by the Finance engine, so the Wealth and Finance tiles, the dashboard addresses and the latest report all fill themselves in. (The published copy on the web can’t ask the Mac for anything, so there those stay quiet.) On the iPhone, it’s the home-screen icon.

The full story — how the system is built, the daily habit, and the monthly money process as a worked example — sits in the next section, The system, end to end.

Four shelves

Cards sit on four shelves, grouped by what you want to do — not by where the code happens to run:

  • FINANCE — the whole monthly money system in one place: the Finance Hub (where the process stands — Mac only), the Spend Dashboard and the Finance Dashboard (the two published chart pages), the latest report in Obsidian (Mac only), and Sam, who runs the month. This shelf is the one place where cards are not all equal — see A front door and the rooms behind it below.
  • EVERYDAY APPS — the web apps you type into: packing, instructions, habits, and the rest.
  • MAC TOOLS — things that only exist on the Mac, like the Timers board.
  • YOUR LINKS — anything else you want a card for.

Four words, used strictly, so the names tell you what a thing is: a Hub is a front door, a Dashboard is a page you read, an App is a tool you type into, a Report is the monthly note in Obsidian.

The two dashboards

The dashboards live on claude.ai, at private addresses. On the Mac (from localhost:7780) the Finance engine hands the hub those addresses itself, and each card admits how many months it lags behind the newest data — the same badge the Finance Hub shows. Anywhere else, tap the pencil on the card once and paste the address; it is stored only on that device and rides along in your backups.

List or grid

The button next to Settings switches the shelf between a list (best on the iPhone) and a grid of tiles (best on the Mac). The hub picks a sensible default for your screen and remembers your choice on each device. Everything works the same in both — the sections just sit in their own panels either way.

Four shelves, four colours. Each shelf writes its name in its own colour — FINANCE indigo, EVERYDAY APPS teal, MAC TOOLS lilac, YOUR LINKS coral — with a rule across the line and a plank of the same colour along its bottom edge, so you can see at a glance which shelf you are looking at when only part of it is on screen.

A front door and the rooms behind it. On the FINANCE shelf the AMS Finance Hub is the front door, and the three things it takes you to — the Spend Dashboard, the Finance Dashboard and the latest report — are nested underneath it. On the Mac, in the grid, the hub runs across the whole width of the shelf and the three sit indented below it in their own block, on smaller tiles. On the phone the same idea takes its own shape: the hub is the big card and the three are small rows stepped in from the left, joined by a thin line. Either way they are drawn quieter than everything else — each keeps its own colour, faded most of the way to the paper, and the calm labels lose their pill (only n months behind keeps its voice, because that one is a warning). Nothing about them changed except how they are drawn — they still open the same pages, and they still carry their own colour, their own icon and their up-to-date badge. Sam stays a full card, because Sam runs the month with you rather than being somewhere the hub sends you.

The latest report away from the Mac. That card is filled in by the Finance engine, which is what knows which note is newest and how to open it. On the iPhone there is nothing to ask, so the card stands with its glyph greyed and MAC ONLY beside it — it keeps its place in the group rather than disappearing, and a tap tells you the report is a Mac errand.

Move one of the three away from the hub in Arrange and it steps back out of the group — no line, no indent, just an ordinary card again, rather than pretending to hang off whatever now sits above it. Repaint the Finance Hub and the line takes its new colour. Sam is untouched throughout: Sam runs the month with you rather than being somewhere the hub sends you.

Make the shelf yours

The Arrange button in the header opens arrange mode: every card grows ▲ ▼ buttons to move it up or down its shelf, and a little palette button that lets you pick the card's colour from a set of calm, matching shades — or send it back to its own colour with one tap. Tap the ✓ when you're happy. Your order and colours are remembered on this device, and they ride along in your backups.

The little version chips

The small number on each web-app card is live — the hub quietly asks each app which version it's running right now, every time you open the hub. So the shelf doubles as a status board: if you just published an update, the new number shows up here. If a chip is missing, the hub couldn't reach that app (offline, or the app doesn't announce a version).

iPhone rule: open apps from their own icons

Each AMS app installed on your home screen keeps its data in its own private box. A link tapped inside the hub app can't open that installed copy — it would open a separate, empty one instead. That's why, when the hub runs as a home-screen app, tapping a web-app card first shows a friendly reminder to use the app's own icon. On the Mac, in Safari, cards simply open in a new tab and everything shares the same data — no reminder needed.

Mac only

Timers, the Finance Hub and the latest report live on the Mac itself, not on the internet, so their cards are marked MAC ONLY or OBSIDIAN. Tapping them on the iPhone does nothing useful — that's expected.

The mini tiles

Across the top sit small tiles, all visible at once — no arrows to press. Each shows one number, in its own colour. On a wide screen they sit in a single row; on the iPhone they lay themselves out two across.

You choose which ones appear. Tap Arrange and every tile grows a small circle in its corner — filled means shown, empty means hidden. Tiles you switch off stay on screen while you are arranging, greyed out, so you can always bring one back. Tap the tick when you are done; your choice is remembered on this device.

  • Wealth · 0 — the very same money, drawn from zero instead. Honest about scale: the filled block shows how much there is, and a year of movement is only a slight tilt along the top. Keep it beside the other one, or keep just the one you prefer.
  • Wealth — your latest total, how it moved since your previous entry, and a small line showing its shape over the past year. The total is the sum of the account balances you type in through the pencil on this tile — see Entering your wealth below. On the Mac, if the budget workbook has a figure for the same month, it appears as a small grey workbook line under your total so you can spot a mismatch; your own entry is always the number that counts. The numbers live only on this device and in your own backup files — never in the hub's public code.
  • Retirement — the days left until 1 July 2028.
  • The year — how much of the year has gone, and how many days are left.
  • Finance — the month being reported, with a dot for each of the four steps (Report · Ledger · Workbook · Dashboard) that fills in as you finish it. Asked live from the Finance engine, so it needs the Mac with the engine running. The tiles are a glance, not a workplace: the little Finance Hub › on this tile takes you to where the work is.
  • Watch later — how many videos are waiting in AMS WatchLater, how long they come to altogether, and how many of them fit into five minutes. Answered by that app's own engine here on the Mac, so it fills in when the engine is running and stays quiet otherwise. It asks for those counts and nothing else — no titles and no links ever leave the app.
  • Apps — how many of your apps answered when the hub asked their version just now. All of them answering means everything is alive; the actual numbers sit on the cards below.
Entering your wealth

Tap the pencil on the Wealth tile. The sheet has three pages, chosen with the buttons at the top:

  • New entry — the date (today, unless you change it) and one row per account. Type each balance in kronor; spaces and dots are fine. Next to a row, the small arrow opens that bank or broker in Safari so you can read the balance, then come back — what you have typed so far is kept, even if the phone reloads the hub in between. The Total at the bottom adds up as you type. A row you leave empty carries its last known balance (shown greyed as last: …) and the entry says which accounts were carried, so a total is never silently short. Save entry stores the snapshot. If that date already has an entry, saving replaces it.
  • Accounts — the list starts empty; add each account with a name and, if you like, the web address of its bank or broker. The pencil renames an account, changes its link or deletes it; ▲ ▼ change the order of the rows. Deleting an account keeps its old balances in past entries — it only stops appearing in new ones.
  • History — every entry, newest first, with its total and what was carried. The pencil reopens an entry so you can correct it; delete removes it. Below the list the hub reports its safety copy: once a day it keeps a copy of your accounts and entries, always holding on to the fullest one it has seen, and can put it back with one tap if something ever goes missing.

The Wealth tiles then show the total of your newest entry, how it moved since the one before, and the shape over the past year — one point per month, the last entry of each month. All of it stays on this device and in your backups; nothing is typed into the hub's public code.

Where everything lives

Every card carries a row of tiny hand-drawn icons showing where that app keeps its stuff. Point at them with the mouse on the Mac — or tap them on the iPhone — and the full story appears in words. The icons:

  • A phone — the app stores everything on the device you use it on (each device has its own copy; that's what the app's backup buttons are for).
  • Phone + Mac + cloud together — AMS Packing is the special one: its catalogue lives on each device and syncs between iPhone and Mac through a Dexie Cloud account.
  • A Mac screen — the app and its data live on the Mac itself.
  • A grid sheet — the Finance Hub doesn't store anything itself; it watches the monthly Excel workbooks and ledgers in the AMS Finance folder in Documents.
  • A gem — Sam, the robot assistant, keeps notes and memory as plain files in the Obsidian vault on the Mac.
  • The apps' code is public on GitHub — but none of your data ever is.

Your links

The bottom shelf is yours: add any web address, file or folder you want a card for. These links are stored only on this device, never in the hub's public code, so private addresses stay private. The pencil on a link edits or deletes it.

How a link card reads. The name you gave it, and under it one small word for what it is — SPREADSHEET, PDF, PICTURE, SLIDES, DOCUMENT, FOLDER, or the site's name for a web link. The long address is not on the card: it lives behind the pencil (and on the Mac it appears if you rest the pointer on the card). Each kind brings its own colour and its own hand-drawn icon — a spreadsheet is green, a PDF red, a folder honey — and a web link keeps a colour picked from its name. The palette in Arrange still overrules all of it whenever you want a particular card a particular colour.

Files and folders on the Mac. The same field also takes a path — for example /Users/you/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox/Triathlon/Training plan.xlsx. In the Finder, right-click the file, hold ⌥ and choose Copy as Pathname, then paste it here. The card gets a document or folder icon, and a tap opens the file in its own app (or the folder in the Finder). This works from the hub on the Mac — the one AMS Finance in the Dock opens at localhost:7780 — because that copy can ask the Finance engine to open things for it. The engine only opens what exists inside your home folder or on an attached disk. On the iPhone, or in the published copy on the web, tapping such a card simply copies the path.

Backups

Under Your data below you can save your links — along with the card order and colours you've chosen, the dashboard addresses, and your wealth accounts and entries — as a small backup file, copy them to the clipboard, or restore a backup. A restore shows its work: it tells you exactly how many links, accounts and entries came back, and if anything goes wrong your current data and your backup file are left untouched. Because wealth entries live on the device you typed them on, a backup is also how you carry them from the iPhone to the Mac.

Updates

The hub checks for a new version of itself when you open it (and whenever you come back to it, at most every 5 minutes). When a newer version is live, a bar appears at the bottom with an Update button — one tap loads it. The Check for app update button below asks immediately.

The system, end to end

Read this the day you come back after weeks away, or when something feels out of place. It says what the system is, how it is built, what you do every day and every month, and how to notice when it needs improving.

1 · What the system is

AMS is the household’s set of small apps, one for each thing worth keeping track of. One of them, the money system, is a real process with a monthly rhythm. The rest are everyday tools. All of it opens from one front door: AMS Main Hub.

Four words, used strictly, so the name of a thing tells you what it is:

  • Hub — a front door. You start there; it shows status and takes you onward. AMS Main Hub is the front door to everything; AMS Finance Hub is the front door to the money process.
  • Dashboard — a page you read. Charts, no typing. The Spend Dashboard and the Finance Dashboard.
  • App — a tool you type into. Packing, Instructions, Tracking, and the rest.
  • Report — the monthly finance note in Obsidian that Sam writes.
2 · How it is built

Three layers. You always enter at the top and go down; nothing skips a layer.

LAYER 1 · THE FRONT DOORAMS Main Hub. The tiles say how things stand; the four shelves take you onward. On the Mac it is what the Dock icon opens (served by the Finance engine at localhost:7780). On the iPhone it is the home-screen icon.
▼
LAYER 2 · THE WORKPLACEAMS Finance Hub (localhost:7780/finance, Mac only). The monthly status board, the KPI cards, the checklist, and one-click tiles for every file and note the process touches. This is where the monthly work happens.
▼
LAYER 3 · SOURCES AND OUTPUTSThe budget workbook (Excel), the monthly ledger, the Obsidian vault (reports, expenses log, wealth log), Sam who does the monthly run in Cowork, and the two dashboards on claude.ai that Sam refreshes from those files.

Behind the scenes, a small helper program on the Mac — the engine — reads the files in layer 3 and shows layers 1 and 2. It starts by itself when the Mac starts; you never see it. If it is off, the tiles say “engine not answering”.

The one-page map of every part, where it runs and where its data lives, is in How this works › Where everything lives.

3 · The daily habit

One habit, same on every device: open AMS Main Hub, read the tiles, tap the card you need.

  • Wealth — the total of your newest entry and its shape over the past year. Typed in through the pencil on the tile, one balance per account; on the Mac the workbook's figure sits under it as a check.
  • Finance — the month being reported, with a dot for each of the four steps. All four filled means nothing is waiting. A missing dot means the Finance shelf is where the work is.
  • Retirement and The year — the calendar, at a glance.

Keep only the tiles you actually glance at: Arrange, then the small circle on each tile. Fewer tiles, faster glance.

On the iPhone the tiles that need the Mac stay quiet, and the dashboard cards show a pencil. That is expected. Tap the pencil once and paste a dashboard’s address if you want it on the phone too; otherwise read the dashboards on the Mac.

4 · The monthly process

The money process closes one month at a time, in the month after it. The Finance Hub carries it: a status board with four checks per month, and a checklist of seven steps that resets on the 1st. Four of the steps tick themselves (⚡) when the hub sees the work is done; three you tick by hand because they need your judgment.

The four checks on the status board, in order:

  1. Report — the month’s report exists in Obsidian.
  2. Ledger — the month has its row in the monthly ledger.
  3. Workbook — the month’s actuals are really written into the budget workbook.
  4. Dashboard — the month is included in the Spend Dashboard.

A finished month is not due straight away: bank statements need a week to settle, so it becomes due on the 8th of the following month (you can change that day in the Finance Hub). Until then the row shows a calm grey “bank statements settling”. From the 8th it turns amber: Due — run this month.

Worked example: closing August 2026. Everything below is what you see and do, in order, in September.
  1. 1 September. The checklist resets — all seven ticks cleared. The status board shows August as “Due 8 Sept · bank statements settling”. The Finance tile on AMS Main Hub reads Aug 2026 · 0 of 4 done, four empty dots. Nothing to do yet.
  2. 8 September or later. August turns amber on the board. Open AMS Main Hub from the Dock, see the empty dots, click AMS Finance Hub on the FINANCE shelf.
  3. Export bank transactions (Swedbank). Download August’s transactions from the bank and save the file in the Imports folder (the Folders tiles at the bottom of the Finance Hub open it). Tick item 1 by hand.
    Board: unchanged. You did the fetching; the hub can’t see the bank.
  4. Log any manual cash expenses. Open the Expenses log tile (Obsidian) and add anything paid in cash or outside the bank export. Tick item 2 by hand.
  5. Run the monthly report with Sam. Open Sam (the Cowork card on the FINANCE shelf) and ask for the August report. Sam reads the export, sorts every row into the categories, asks you about anything unclear, writes the report into Obsidian, and adds August’s row to the ledger.
    Board: Report ✓ Ledger ✓. Item 3 ticks itself ⚡. The KPI cards switch to August’s income, expenses, net and savings rate. The Finance tile shows two dots.
  6. Resolve flagged merchants and name ticket buys. During the run Sam flags merchants it could not place and asks which multi-year “ticket” a big purchase belongs to (a holiday, a bike). Decide them. Tick item 4 by hand — that judgment is yours, not a file the hub can check.
  7. Write actuals to the budget workbook. Ask Sam to write August’s actuals into the workbook’s August column.
    Board: Workbook ✓. Item 5 ticks itself ⚡. The Finance Dashboard card now says 1 month behind — it hasn’t caught up with the new column yet. Three dots.
  8. Refresh the Spend Dashboard, then the Finance Dashboard. Ask Sam to refresh both. Same links as before; only the numbers change.
    Board: Dashboard ✓, August flips to teal Complete. Items 6 and 7 tick themselves ⚡. Both dashboard cards say UP TO DATE. The Finance tile reads Aug 2026 · all done, four dots filled.
  9. Read. Open the Spend Dashboard from the FINANCE shelf: which categories ran hot, the biggest merchants, Sam’s observations. Then the Finance Dashboard: net worth, budget vs. actual by month, the runway to 2038. If a figure surprises you, the report in Obsidian (the Latest report card) has the row-level story.
  10. Done until October. A month is closed when its row says Complete and the Finance tile shows four dots. On 1 October the checklist resets and September waits for the 8th.

The whole run takes one sitting once the export is in. If you get interrupted, the board and the checklist remember exactly where you stopped — that is the point of them.

5 · When something looks wrong
  • Tiles say “engine not answering”, dashboard cards show pencils on the Mac. You are looking at the web copy of the hub, or the engine is off. Open the hub from the Dock icon (it must open localhost:7780). If that page won’t load, double-click AMS Finance.app in Documents › 01 Leisure › 30 App Development › AMS Finance — that starts the engine and opens the hub.
  • A dashboard card says “N months behind”. Not an error: the workbook or the report is newer than the dashboard. Ask Sam to refresh that dashboard; the badge clears on the next reload.
  • A ⚡ item didn’t tick. Reload the Finance Hub (↻ Refresh). Items tick when the hub sees the matching check go green; if the check is still red, the work isn’t finished as far as the files show.
  • A version chip is missing on a card. The hub couldn’t reach that app just now — offline, or the app is being published. Harmless.
  • An “Update” bar appears. A newer hub is live. Tap Update. On the Mac the hub is read straight from its folder, so there the newest version is always what you see after a pull in GitHub Desktop.
  • Something else. Open Sam or Claude and describe what you see — a screenshot says more than a paragraph.
6 · Keeping it tidy

The disorder crept in because things were added one at a time without a place to put them. These rules give every new thing its place:

  • One front door. Nothing gets its own Dock icon. New things become a card on AMS Main Hub.
  • Every card goes on the shelf that says what it is for. Money things on FINANCE, tools on EVERYDAY APPS, Mac-only helpers on MAC TOOLS, everything else in YOUR LINKS.
  • Name things with the four words. A new chart page is a Dashboard; a new tool is an App. Never a second name for the same thing.
  • One dashboard per question. Before asking for a new chart page, ask whether an existing one could carry the answer.
  • Every release writes one line in its app’s version log. That log is the memory of the system — it is what you read after weeks away.
  • Files stay where the map says. The budget workbook, ledger and reports keep their folders; the Finance Hub’s tiles point at them, so a moved file shows up as a red check.
7 · The quarterly review

Once a quarter — a good moment is right after a month closes — open AMS Main Hub and answer these honestly. Each “no” or “never” is an improvement waiting to be made; tell Sam or Claude and it becomes a change.

  1. Which tiles did I actually glance at this quarter? Hide the rest with Arrange.
  2. Which cards did I never click? Move them down their shelf, or ask whether the app has earned its place.
  3. Did any checklist item stay unticked for a reason other than forgetting? Then the step is unclear or too big — split it, or reword it in the Finance Hub.
  4. Which step of the monthly run took the longest? Could Sam do more of it, or could the hub check it instead of me?
  5. Did I look for something and not find it on the shelf? Give it a card, on the right shelf.
  6. Does every name still mean what the four words say? Rename anything that drifted.
  7. Did a dashboard answer a question I stopped asking? Retire it rather than keep refreshing it.

Write the answers in the Obsidian vault, or simply tell Claude. Small, regular corrections are what keep a home-grown system feeling like home.
